:is() and :where()

Same selector lists, different specificity: :where() contributes zero, :is() inherits its most specific argument.

:where() baseline typography

This paragraph has class lead — one class (0,1,0) overrides the :where() base (0,0,0) even though the base comes later in the sheet.

Same for note-text: utility classes always win without specificity wars, because :where() adds nothing to the cascade weight.

This plain paragraph keeps the :where() baseline gray.

:is() raises the stakes

:is(#featured) contributes ID-level specificity (1,0,0) + class — so this paragraph turns gold and a simple class cannot easily beat it.

:where(article) :where(h2)(0,0,0)
.lead utility(0,1,0) → wins
:is(#featured) p(1,0,1)
plain article p(0,0,1)

Rule of thumb: wrap forgiving lists in :where() for overridable defaults, :is() when you want grouped intent to carry weight. Both are forgiving — invalid selectors inside never invalidate the whole list.
