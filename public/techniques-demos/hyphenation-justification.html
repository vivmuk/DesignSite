The Narrow Column Problem

Typography exists to honor content. When columns narrow beyond roughly forty characters, justification without hyphenation tears holes in the texture: word spacing balloons, rivers of white run diagonally through the paragraph, and the reader's eye stumbles at every line turn. Hyphenation re-knits the fabric. The dictionary breaks long words at syllable seams, the justification algorithm redistributes the remainder, and the grey of the column returns to an even hum — the quiet signature of professionally set newsprint, now available in the browser with nothing more than hyphens: auto and a lang attribute.
