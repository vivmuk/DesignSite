Field notes · issue 12The quiet layer

What a page remembers after you leave.

On texture, rhythm, and the details nobody screenshots.

The first thing a reader feels is not the headline. It is the measure — how far the eye travels before it is asked to turn. Set that line too long and the page becomes a chore; too short and it stutters. A drop cap buys back the altitude the opening needs.

Typography is the only material a website is actually made of. Everything else — the photography, the gradients, the motion — is a guest in the house that type built. That is why a single well-set paragraph can carry more authority than a hero section.

“Hierarchy is a promise that the page keeps or breaks.”

Notice the small caps on the first line, the hanging punctuation, the rules that behave like breathing marks. None of these are visible as features. They are visible only as ease.
